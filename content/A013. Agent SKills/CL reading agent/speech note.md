# Slide 10 (15 Seconds)

To guide the students' reading interaction, we developed thirteen reading log questions based on Lewison et al.'s framework. These structured questions prompt students on various critical dimensions, from identifying missing voices in a text to proposing actionable social plans.

# Slide 12 (45 Seconds)
![[Pasted image 20260508233400.png]]
While the core features of our AI agent's design. The first is Contextualization, grounded in Transactional Theory that the af the student’s self-selected reading materials, we ensure critical inquiry is grounded in their personal interests. The agent can read uploaded PDFs or scrape content directly from a URL to use as the basis for conversation. Additionally, we incorporated Immediate Feedback based on Self-Determination Theory. For instance, the AI initiates an engaging dialogue by validating their article choice and gently asking what motivated them to select it, creating an authentic transaction.

# Slide 13 (30 Seconds)
![[Pasted image 20260508233430.png]]
Next, we utilized Adaptive Probing, rooted in Social Constructivism. The agent pushes students within their Zone of Proximal Development to move beyond surface-level answers and interrogate hidden values or textual "blind spots". Furthermore, guided by Reflective Practice, the agent performs a Synthesis. It reviews all the questions asked during the session and generates an open-ended synthesis question to engage students in much deeper reflection.

# Slide 15 (30 Seconds)
![[Pasted image 20260508233459.png]]
Finally, diving deeper into the workflow, the AI operates as a supportive companion that prioritizes the student's ideas. The interaction sequence is highly structured: it asks for article information, processes the thirteen reading questions, summarizes the thoughts to confirm interpretation, and then synthesizes the discussion with a concluding question. It ends by generating a final summary and expressing appreciation for the student's effort.
