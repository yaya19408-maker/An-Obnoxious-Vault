# Yang, W., & Lin, C. (2025). Translanguaging with generative AI in EFL writing: Students’ practices and perceptions. 
_Journal of Second Language Writing_, _67_, 101181.

## Keywords
Translanguaging, GAI, EFL students, Writing development, Language learning

## Purpose
how low to mid proficiency level Chinese students use ERNIE bot (文心一言) to translanguaging

## RQs
1. how to interact via translanguaging
2. perception of GAI writing

## Lit rev
1. Garcia & Li (2014): use bilingual or multilingual resources to deliver complex thoughts
2. Sasaki (2023): L1 is peak at EFL writing and use AI to reach
3. Velasco & Garcia (2014); Zheng & Drybrough (2023): translanguaging $\in$ SRL for writing
4. Tseng & Warchauer (2023): AI-assisted language learning $\rightarrow$ AI literacy

## Methodology
1. Framework: AI literacy $\ni$ understand, access, prompt, corroborate, incorporate
2. Design: qualitative, 100-min semi-structured focus group interview after 1-semester intervention
	1. IQ 1: how & why use different languages
	2. IQ 2: how AI assisted them
	3. IQ 3: what challenges encountered
3. Materials: interaction logs (links) & written texts
4. Analysis: thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006)

# Findings
1. Praxis: active translanguaging by solving complicated problems using L1 and multimodal resources for inspirations
> “_Our English proficiency is not high enough, so we may not construct a grammatically correct sentence if asking AI directly in English. And if I ask it in broken English, it might not understand me either. Asking AI in Chinese allows it to understand what I want to ask, helping it better understand my needs.” (Xie, interview excerpt)_

but some reported ineffective instances
> Shi noted that ERNIE Bot sometimes failed to produce expressions in English when prompted in Chinese. As Chen explained: “You have to constantly ask it (ERNIE Bot) to ‘generate in English’.”

2. Perception: leverage entire language repertoire throughout the writing process.
> _“After receiving the essay prompt, I usually start by thinking in Chinese about what the topic is and what content I can write about. If there are terms in the prompt that I don't understand, I would refer to online dictionaries, or now, AI to translate them into Chinese, and then think about how to write the essay.” (Chen, interview excerpt)_

> _“If I can’t come up with any ideas, I sometimes ask AI to create a mind map and it will list out many possible points in Chinese, and then I’ll pick some points that I like to write about.” (Yang, interview excerpt)_

but some reported limitations on their thinking (anchoring effect in psychology)
> other participants reported forming the content of their essays initially in Chinese. They believed that brainstorming in Chinese imposed fewer limitations on their thinking, as they “only had a much smaller vocabulary in English compared to Chinese” (Shi, interview excerpt). For example, when they wrote the essays about addiction to technology, they brainstormed the potential harms of overusing technology in Chinese, and later translated them into English. Therefore, content knowledge was often elicited in their native language before being expressed in English writing.

3. Actual model use: did not follow
> The data also revealed that students did not follow a straightforward “ask-answer-adopt” model when transforming their ideas into English with AI’s assistance. Instead, they frequently moved between languages during their negotiations with AI, showing a dynamic cognitive process.

4. Emotions: mixed $\ni$ satisfaction, frustration, anxiety, reliance

Satisfaction:
> _“When it pointed out and corrected my grammar errors, I felt like, ‘Wow! Awesome! It could really be a teacher and help me correct my mistakes.” (Xie, interview excerpt)_

Sense of fulfillment:
> As Chen pointed out, AI could help them to write sentences “in a more advanced way” that they could not achieve on their own. With the help of AI, they were able to produce expressions in English beyond their current language level, making them feel less inefficient in English writing.

Anxiety:
> _“I become more anxious, feeling that it (ERNIE Bot) writes so well. I used to think that my own sentences were already well-written, but it makes me realize that in comparison, my writing is not that good.” (Liu, interview excerpt)_

Sense of cognitive gap:
> Liu recognized the gap between her current English proficiency and that of a more advanced writer, which heightened her anxiety about English writing.

Fear of overreliance:
> _“I think the ideas it (ERNIE Bot) gave me were quite good. Sometimes I might just be too lazy to think about the topic on my own.” (Chen, interview excerpt)_
